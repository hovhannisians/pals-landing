Every pal has its own computer. That's what lets it keep working while you do something else: it isn't borrowing your screen, your mouse or your browser.

## Where the computer runs

Every pal's computer runs in the cloud, on [E2B](https://e2b.dev). Each pal gets a Linux machine of its own with a real Chrome, kept apart from every other pal. You can close the lid and the pal's computer carries on.

The pal's **Computer** tab shows its computer and lets you watch it work.

## The browser

Each pal has its own Chrome profile. When you sign it in to a site, it stays signed in, just like your own browser does.

The pal finds buttons, links and fields by the text on the page. That works on most sites, but not on things drawn on a canvas, like some maps and games.

## Watching and taking over

Open a pal's **Computer** tab to watch its browser live.

When a pal hits a login, a captcha or a payment step, it asks you to take over. Choose **Take over**, do the step yourself in the live view, then choose **Hand back to** the pal. It carries on from where you left it.

You can take over at any time, not only when asked.

## Access to your own computer

In the Mac and Windows apps, you can also give a pal access to your own computer from its **Computer** tab, for jobs that really need your machine. It can then run commands there, not just on its own computer. It asks before every command by default, and you can turn the access off at any time.

This option doesn't exist on hosted Pals or in Robi Chat.

## Payments and bookings

For a booking or a purchase, the site needs a card already saved in your account there, or you take over for the payment step. Pals never handles card numbers itself. Buying something is the kind of action that needs your approval by default.
